# Personal Positioning

## Why this exists

This is the personal layer of the casebook. It is not a role-specific application pitch and it should not name a target job family as the headline.

The purpose is to give Nidhi a consistent way to introduce herself to a hiring manager, engineering leader, product leader, developer-facing leader, or technical community leader, while allowing the examples to change depending on the company's needs.

## Core positioning

I am a senior software engineer who likes working at the intersection of difficult systems, emerging technology, and the people who have to build with those systems.

Over the years I have moved from building applications to thinking more deeply about the larger engineering experience around them: how developers understand a system, how they find the right path, how complex technical decisions get explained, how reusable knowledge is created, and how we know whether a system actually works in practice.

More recently, I have been applying that way of thinking to AI and LLM systems, especially context engineering, evaluation, regression automation, and production readiness.

What I enjoy most is taking something that feels complicated or ambiguous, finding the real engineering problem underneath it, building enough of the system to make the idea concrete, and then explaining the decisions so that another engineer can actually use, challenge, and extend the work.

That is the thread connecting my engineering work, technical writing, reference implementations, and the platform case studies in this repository.

## 30-second pitch

I am a senior software engineer with a strong interest in the space between building systems and helping other engineers build effectively with them. I enjoy taking complex engineering problems, making the architecture and trade-offs concrete, and turning that into runnable examples and clear technical material. Lately I have been focusing heavily on AI systems, evaluation, context engineering, and developer workflows, while also exploring the same engineering patterns across platforms like banking, retail, operations, streaming, healthcare, and technical assessment.

## 60-second pitch

I have spent my career building software systems, and over time I have become increasingly interested in a broader problem: not just how we build a system, but how we make it understandable, usable, extensible, and reliable for the engineers working with it.

That has led me toward work around platform thinking, developer journeys, architecture decisions, reusable technical knowledge, and more recently AI systems. I am particularly interested in the parts that are easy to underestimate—context, evaluation, failure modes, developer friction, and the difference between something that looks correct and something that actually works.

I am building a technical body of work around those ideas using realistic platforms such as payments and lending, retail, operations, music, healthcare, streaming, and coding assessment. I use them to explore the system design, implementation choices, failure modes, and developer experience together rather than treating them as separate concerns.

## 90-second pitch

I am a senior software engineer who has spent more than a decade building software and gradually moving closer to the problems that sit above individual features.

The work that interests me most is where there is a complicated technical system, a real developer or user journey around it, and some ambiguity about how the two should fit together.

I like starting with the problem rather than the technology: who is trying to do what, where they get stuck, what context is missing, what can fail, and what constraints the system has to respect. From there I work through architecture, implementation, validation, and the trade-offs that make the design practical.

AI has made this particularly interesting to me. I have been exploring context engineering, LLM evaluation, regression automation, and production AI as systems problems rather than simply model problems. A useful AI feature is not just a good prompt or a fluent response; it needs the right context, tools, evaluation, failure handling, evidence, and feedback loop.

I am also deliberately broadening the examples I work through. The same engineering patterns show up in very different products: idempotency in payments and orders, event-driven state in streaming and operations, ranking and feedback loops in music and retail, evidence and human approval in healthcare, and trustworthy evaluation in coding assessment.

What I am looking for next is a place where I can keep doing that kind of work: building technically credible things, making difficult systems easier for other engineers to understand and use, and turning real developer problems into better technical experiences and better products.

That has become a big theme in my work—from platform architecture and developer workflows to AI evaluation and context engineering. ## How to adapt the story

Keep the personal pitch stable. Change the evidence.

| Audience / company need | Lead with | Then show |
|---|---|---|
| Developer tooling / technical platform | developer journey + platform thinking | golden paths, service discovery, reference implementations |
| Coding / assessment products | challenge quality + evaluation | assessment platform, LLM evaluation, realistic task environments |
| Fintech / banking | correctness + state + resilience | payments, ledger, fraud, loans, reconciliation |
| Retail / commerce | scale + personalization + consistency | catalog, inventory, search, checkout, recommendation |
| Operations / logistics | changing constraints + execution | dispatch, scheduling, re-planning, event-driven state |
| Music / content discovery | ranking + feedback + experimentation | recommendation, cold start, freshness, feedback loops |
| Healthcare / regulated systems | evidence + privacy + human control | FHIR, retrieval, clinical workflows, approvals |
| Streaming / media | event systems + scale + reliability | ingestion, stream processing, playback telemetry, recommendations |
| AI-first company | context + evaluation + production readiness | LLM evaluation, context engineering, agent/tool interfaces |

## What not to say

Avoid making the headline about a job title, a desire to "move into" a function, or a generic passion for developer communities.

Avoid saying that an external company's architecture is personal work. Label public examples as references and personal work as personal work.

Avoid leading with a long technology inventory. Start with the engineering problem and use technology to prove depth.

## Evidence behind the positioning

The casebook should make every part of this pitch verifiable:

- **Build:** real repositories and runnable reference implementations.
- **Think:** architecture cases, trade-offs, state models, failure modes.
- **Explain:** layered 30-second, 2-minute, and deep-dive material.
- **Evaluate:** test strategy, LLM-as-a-Judge design, evidence and failure analysis.
- **Scale knowledge:** patterns, templates, reusable technical material.
- **Understand developers:** end-to-end journey modeling and friction analysis.

The pitch is the invitation. The case studies are the proof.
